Inside the hangar

Three distillers.
One legacy.

A German-born lawyer with a 65-gallon pot still. A nuclear engineer who turned up with a bottle of homemade whiskey as his résumé. A grad student who worked out he was spending his whole teaching salary on booze. Forty years later, the still is still running.

Founder · 1982

Jörg Rupf

When Jörg founded St. George in 1982, there was no such thing as "craft distilling" in America. When he ran his first batch of eau de vie on a 65-gallon pot still, he didn't just start a distillery — he started a movement, and then helped a generation of other distillers start theirs.

Read more
Master Distiller · 1996

Lance Winters

Lance applied for a job here in 1996 and his résumé was a bottle of whiskey he'd distilled in his garage. His creative streak built on Jörg's technique to make spirits that were the first of their kind — and are now landmarks of American artisan distilling.

Read more
Head Distiller · 2005

Dave Smith

Dave was focused on grad school when he took a day job here. Now he's a master of the craft. As Lance puts it: "Dave took our single malt, which was good, and turned it great." He sees a spirit like a screenplay — it needs bartenders and drinkers to direct it.

Read more
Eau de vie

French for water of life — and the philosophy behind every bottle we fill. Imagine cramming thirty pounds of fruit into one 750ml bottle. That's eau de vie. Whether we're making whiskey, gin, vodka, or something completely off-the-wall, we approach it the same way: start with the best raw material, and get as much of its flavor into the glass as the still will allow.
